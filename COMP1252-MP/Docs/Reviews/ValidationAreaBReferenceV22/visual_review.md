# Area B independent imagery review

The independent images strengthen the interpretation of forest loss at the
lower opening. They show historical tree crowns inside the reviewed lower
interior and a matching opening after the change. The exact forest extent on
6 July 2018 remains unresolved. The images also distinguish the control
candidates. Controls 1 and 2 have stronger forest support than control 3,
where an opening is visible by 2020. Class approval remains pending.

## Dated observations

| Capture | Reference | What is visible on the fixed crop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 14 June 2010 | DigitalGlobe WorldView-2, 0.5 m sampling, 10.2 m declared horizontal accuracy | Recognizable crowns and canopy texture occupy the lower target and all three control squares. The visible stand texture varies across the target. |
| 5 July 2018 | Landsat 8, 15 m panchromatic and 30 m color | Bright diffuse cloud-like cover obscures the lower target and much of controls 2 and 3. Control 1 remains dark but the coarse pixels leave its class ambiguous. |
| 30 July 2018 | Landsat 8, same display method | A broad pale lower block with a narrower northward connector aligns with the target. The control area to the west remains dark. This independently supports visibility of the broad opening by 30 July. |
| 6 August 2018 | Landsat 8, same display method | Diffuse brightness and weak contrast cross the crop. The target boundary and full-square cover are indeterminate. |
| 15 August 2018 | Landsat 8, same display method | A similar broad opening remains visible, with coarse edges and some diffuse background brightness. |
| 6 November 2018 selection | Google Earth historical imagery, Maxar Technologies | The lower opening is visible in the eastern portion of the dated selection. The viewer attributes a mixture of older and November imagery. A visible seam limits dating across the western controls. |
| 31 August 2020 | Maxar WorldView-3, 0.5 m sampling, 5 m declared horizontal accuracy | The lower opening shows open ground with rows and a straight lower boundary. Controls 1 and 2 still show canopy. Open ground occupies a substantial western part of control 3. |

The two WorldView acquisitions each have metadata coverage of the complete
crop. The three sampled 2018 Wayback releases (27 June, 25 July and 15 August)
link to the same **14 June 2010** capture. Map release dates and source dates
are recorded separately. Each fixed Landsat render locks to one named
acquisition. The source previews support visual comparison of broad features.
Cloud-like areas are visual observations from these previews.

## Control dispositions

| Fixed square | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Control 1, row 2 column 2 | Strongest provisional candidate. Crowns occupy the square in both WorldView views. Coarse 2018 views leave whole-square forest continuity unresolved. |
| Control 2, row 22 column 2 | Provisional candidate. Both WorldView views show canopy. Its lower-left corner is closer to the later opening edge, where source positional accuracy matters. |
| Control 3, row 42 column 2 | Unsuitable as a long-term stable reference. The 2020 opening crosses the western part of the square. Its status during the July–August 2018 window remains unresolved. |

All six reference layers were opened plain first and then checked against the
unchanged target, eroded interior and full control squares. The archival
imagery supplies historical forest and later clearing evidence. Landsat
corroborates the broad target shape but cannot resolve the Sentinel-2 edges
or certify whole-square forest. The site-specific OpenAerialMap search
returned zero acquisitions from April through November 2018.
[OpenAerialMap query](https://api.imagery.hotosm.org/stac/search?bbox=35.3073028%2C50.5715234%2C35.3166156%2C50.5774538&datetime=2018-04-01T00%3A00%3A00Z%2F2018-11-01T00%3A00%3A00Z&limit=10)
